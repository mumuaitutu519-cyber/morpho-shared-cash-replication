# R35 distribution and functional-form audit
Requested after R34. This is an exploratory, finite robustness exercise, not a new confirmatory hypothesis.

1. Recompute all main descriptive tables on their original samples, reporting N, mean, sample SD, minimum, P25, median, P75 and maximum. Retain raw amounts, zero-flow days and verified large transactions. Report skewness, zero share, P99, largest-observation value share and transformed skewness in supporting data.
2. Compare exactly three representations of the daily treasury deposit, withdrawal and net outcomes on the original 319 dates and original calendar/control design: raw million-USDC outcomes; 1/99-percent winsorized gross deposit and withdrawal outcomes with net and its lag rebuilt; ln(1+USDC amount) for nonnegative gross outcomes and asinh(net USDC) for signed net flows and its own lag. The log transformation therefore has a fixed 1-USDC offset/scale and is not mislabeled ln(x) or a uniform percentage elasticity.
3. Keep existing utilization, premium, logged assets, return, volatility, month and weekday regressors unchanged. The sensitivity concerns flow outcomes and their own lag, not blanket winsorization of indicators or bounded ratios. Use existing HAC7/HAC14 inference, store every result, compare original utilization/premium focal coefficients, and retain cumulative multiplicity reporting.
4. No removal of observations or changes to cash accounting, funding bounds, transaction amounts or execution requests. Owner-panel log claim and vault log assets already exist; binary actions need no logarithm. Do not choose among these transformations by significance.
5. Present the full raw distribution in main tables; add compact transformation sensitivity and interpretation to the appendix, with clear main-text pointer. Deliver updated English PDF/LaTeX and focused replay inputs/results.
