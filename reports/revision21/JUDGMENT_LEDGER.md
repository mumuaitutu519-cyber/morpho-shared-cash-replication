# R21 evidence and interpretation decisions

This is a response to a second simulated review, not an actual editorial decision.

| Decision | Evidence | Permitted interpretation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main economic object is holder realizable liquidity | First-deposit manifests, account shares, historical capacity | A linked measurement across records for one account | No same-token or same-cohort lifecycle claim |
| Separate all-facility and vault amounts | 137 PSM + 94 vault deposits; exact raw joins | Total facility funding 890.088–907.731m; vault funding 471.788–489.891m | PSM is outside the vault liquidity test; subset endpoints are marginal |
| Reconcile rather than source-tag stock | 319 daily stock equations; 1.532277m-share residual | Verified cohort is 8.22% of gross deposit turnover | Ending source share and full fee decomposition remain unidentified |
| Interpret 25% as a large immediate scenario | 12,321 successful transactions and 319 days | Typical transaction is 0.047% of prior close; daily totals can exceed 25% | Prior close differs from pre-transaction stock; success is not latent demand |
| Quantify common-cash duplication | 554 raw portfolio positions; 46 allocation LPs | Separately counted cash exceeds selected-group static upper bound | Vault portfolios are market holders; this is not all terminal owners or simultaneous execution |
| Show size mechanically | Fixed observed portfolio weights, market state and scaled claim | The selected large case is mechanically more constrained | No equilibrium change, institution-level causal inference or representative population |
| Explain recovery | Exact W/C decomposition + common-market cash records | Capacity contributes 65.55pp, claim reduction 12.48pp; common cash explains 99.08% of capacity increase | Selected minimum endpoint; final 2.019m capacity component left unallocated |
| Preserve diagnostic regressions | Entire old Appendix N, tables, equations | Fixed previous designs and imprecise direct contrasts remain | No search for new significance |
| Reviewer reproducibility | Local evidence ZIP and offline replays | Raw checks for central funding, states, account events and new comparisons | Not every incident appendix; no public repository ID or independent reacquisition |

## Closest lending source inspected

Kanis Saengchote (2023), “Decentralized lending and its users: Insights from Compound,” *Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money*, 87, 101807. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intfin.2023.101807.

Inspected published introduction, protocol/account-liquidity discussion and concentration/interconnection discussion (published PDF pp. 1–2, 9, 25–26). Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1042443123000756. Public full paper: https://tdri.or.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/02-Decentralized-lending-and-its-users-Insights-from-Compound.pdf.

It supports acknowledging existing transaction-level research on lending users, leveraged investment, concentration and interconnected participants. It does not establish the new paper's cohort funding bounds, pooled-stock source attribution, shared-cash allocation amount or the chosen withdrawal fractions. Those claims rely on the new paper's own records. The manuscript's contribution is stated on these measurement margins, without a claim to discovering concentration risk.

All new analyses were specified at revision stage in ANALYSIS_PLAN.md before calculation; they are not described as preregistered. Five request fractions, four size factors, all 319 dates and all 46 weekly dates are retained. No new calls to the stopped RPC endpoints were made.
